# Market and customer research

1. Define the buyer and job to be done before sizing the market. Segment by workflow, purchase trigger, organization size and geography where relevant.
2. Collect concrete public demand signals: repeated detailed complaints, job or procurement postings, documented purchases and existing paid workarounds.
3. Separate what customers say, what they demonstrably do and what they demonstrably pay. Label inferred demand.
4. Identify decision-makers, current budgets, procurement friction and who bears a failed automation's cost. Do not infer personal contact details or collect private leads.
5. Estimate market size bottom-up from reachable customers and a plausible offer. Show assumptions and source dates; a broad industry TAM is not the user's attainable revenue.
6. Compare segments on pain, urgency, accessibility, expected delivery effort and evidence quality. Treat desk-research findings as hypotheses for validation.
7. Report the actual research conducted. Never imply that interviews, surveys, sales, retention measurements or paid pilots occurred if they did not.

Use `ai-opportunity-validation` for economics and a small experiment. Preparing interview questions is allowed; contacting people, launching a survey or spending money requires separate authorization.
