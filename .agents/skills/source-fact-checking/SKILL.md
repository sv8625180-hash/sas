# Source fact-checking

1. Break the claim into checkable assertions, including who, what, when, population, units and comparison baseline.
2. Trace each assertion to its original source. Verify the source exists, the quoted passage says what is claimed, and the date/version is relevant.
3. Label the evidence: direct observation, official statement, self-report, independent reporting, preprint, peer-reviewed study, estimate or opinion.
4. Identify common provenance. Ten reposts of one founder's revenue claim are still one self-report. Testimonials and screenshots alone do not prove profit, retention or typical outcomes.
5. For consequential assertions, seek independent corroboration and disconfirmation. If only one authoritative source is available, say so rather than fabricate agreement.
6. Investigate disagreements: different dates, populations, definitions, gross/net figures, currencies or incentives. Preserve unresolved conflicts.
7. Produce a verdict such as supported, partly supported, contradicted or unverified, with precise source links and limitations. Do not attach invented confidence percentages.

For studies, check sample selection, controls, causal inference, measurement, conflicts of interest and generalizability. For businesses, distinguish revenue, profit, cash flow and extraordinary success stories from typical results.

Incluye un ledger de evidencia con afirmación, fuente, fecha, extracto, tipo de evidencia, contradicción y límite. Respeta fuentes bloqueadas; nunca cambies credenciales, identidad o acceso para evadir restricciones.
