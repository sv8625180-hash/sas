# Validate an AI business opportunity

Use after or alongside `deep-web-research`. The objective is to find testable commercial opportunities, not attractive-sounding ideas or guaranteed returns.

## Establish the user's constraints

Identify region and legal context, language, available hours, skills, capital, existing audience or customer access, and risk tolerance. If unknown, compare a few explicit scenarios rather than inventing a personal profile or exact forecast.

## Look for a newly viable solution to a verified problem

For each candidate, describe the buyer, painful workflow, present workaround, triggering event, proposed offer and why it may be timely. Verify the new capability in current official documentation and tests; a release announcement alone is not evidence that it works reliably in production.

Check direct competitors, substitutes, agencies, internal manual work, open-source options and reasons earlier attempts failed. Use "underserved segment" or "differentiation hypothesis," not "nobody is doing it."

Search demand-side evidence separately from supply-side hype. Useful signals include repeated detailed customer complaints, relevant job or procurement postings, disclosed purchasing decisions, paid pilots and observable retention. Likes, viral posts and affiliate rankings are weak proxies for willingness to pay.

## Build an evidence-backed opportunity card

Incluye una ficha autónoma de oportunidad con comprador, problema, evidencia, alternativas, costes, riesgos, supuestos y nivel de confianza de cada afirmación material.

Evaluate:

1. **Pain and buyer:** urgency, frequency, cost of the current problem and decision-maker.
2. **Willingness to pay:** demonstrated spending or a testable offer; distinguish an interview from a purchase.
3. **Distribution:** a realistic channel the user can access, acquisition effort and sales cycle.
4. **Economics:** price minus API/model costs, hosting, human review, acquisition, refunds, support, payment fees and applicable taxes. Record assumptions separately from measured costs.
5. **Feasibility:** actual model performance, required integrations, failure handling, reliability and human-in-the-loop work.
6. **Risk:** privacy, intellectual property, consumer protection, regulated activities, contractual limits and vendor dependence.
7. **Differentiation:** workflow expertise, service quality, distribution, legitimate data access or integration depth; not simply a different prompt.

Provide low/base/high scenarios with explicit assumptions, not a precise earnings prediction. Cite success claims as self-reported unless independently verified. A business generating revenue can still lose money.

## Recommend a small falsifiable experiment

For promising candidates, define a 7–14 day validation plan, maximum authorized spend, success threshold and stop criterion. Prefer interviews, a clearly described service, an opt-in landing page or a small manually delivered paid pilot before building a platform.

Do not contact people, scrape personal leads, spend money, launch ads or create accounts without specific authorization. Do not suggest impersonation, spam, fake reviews, deceptive AI content or unlicensed use of private data.

Conclude with a ranked shortlist, the evidence supporting it, what would invalidate it and the next smallest authorized test. Explain that research reduces uncertainty; it cannot guarantee large profits or prove worldwide novelty.
