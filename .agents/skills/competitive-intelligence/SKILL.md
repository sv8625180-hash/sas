# Competitive intelligence

1. Search by customer problem as well as product category. Include agencies, spreadsheets, internal work, open-source alternatives and "do nothing."
2. Verify each competitor's offer and current status at the original source. Record URLs and dates for prices, units, minimum commitments, trials and limitations.
3. Build a comparison covering buyer, workflow, delivery model, distribution, positioning, integrations and evidence of customer outcomes.
4. Look for recurring complaints, neglected segments and workflow gaps. Cross-check reviews' recency, independence and sampling bias.
5. Investigate failed or discontinued attempts and why the customer may prefer a substitute. Do not treat an unindexed competitor as nonexistent.
6. Propose differentiation hypotheses tied to observed gaps, then define what evidence would disprove them.

Use public, lawful sources only. Do not impersonate customers, seek confidential competitor data, create trial accounts, contact companies or scrape private workspaces without authorization. Do not fabricate market shares, revenue or competitive scores.
